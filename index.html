Research

Formation and signaling of electrophilic fatty acids

Fatty acid metabolites with an alpha, beta-unsaturated carbonyl were once seen as inactive end products. We showed that they signal. They act without G protein-coupled receptors, suppress NF-kB inflammatory signaling, and activate the Nrf2 antioxidant response.

  • We showed that 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase (15-PGDH) oxidizes hydroxylated omega-3 fatty acids into electrophiles.
  • We test whether lipoxin A4 forms in macrophages and whether it and its 15-oxo metabolite signal through FPR2 or through receptor-independent routes.
  • We study how nitro-fatty acids redirect carbon flow through central metabolism in macrophages to promote resolution of inflammation.

Electrophiles as novel therapeutics

Nitro-oleic acid (NO2-OA, also called CP-6) improved lung function in a mouse model of obese allergic airway disease, with changes in host and microbial metabolism.

CP-6 is now in a double-blind, crossover clinical trial in people with obesity and asthma. Each participant receives placebo and CP-6, each for 6 weeks, with a 4-week washout between them. The trial measures lung function with FEV1 and PC20.

Systemic metabolism and lung function

Our CP-6 work raised a broader question about how host and microbial metabolism affect the lung in obesity. We linked gut microbial composition and function to lung function in obese allergic airway disease. Nitroalkene fatty acids also change bile acid metabolism alongside lung function.

The liver metabolizes fatty acids and bile acids, so changes in the liver alter the lipid signals that reach the lung. In lean and obese mouse models of allergic asthma, we measure liver cytochrome P450 enzymes, bile acids, and lipid mediators such as epoxyeicosatrienoic acids alongside lung function.
